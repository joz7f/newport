# How to Make Your Designs Feel Less AI-Generated

AI is everywhere in tech right now. And with that comes a lot of anxiety about AI taking over creative and technical jobs.

But I think there is a different problem we should be talking about: AI is making too many designs look the same.

As AI becomes a bigger part of the design process, companies are encouraging designers to use it to save time. That makes sense on the surface. But if we start using AI to replace the thinking behind a design, we risk losing something much more important than time: personality.


## The problem

Most AI tools are trained on enormous amounts of existing design patterns, interfaces, and visual systems. When we ask them to generate something without giving them enough direction, they tend to fall back on familiar patterns.

And when everyone uses the same tools, those familiar patterns start appearing everywhere.

The result?

Different companies can end up with similar layouts, similar design systems, similar interactions, and even similar visual language. The designs may look polished, but they don't necessarily feel like they belong to a specific brand.

That is a problem because brand identity is one of the things that helps a company stand out.

A user should be able to interact with a product and feel that it belongs to a particular company. The design should have a personality. It should reflect the decisions, values, and thinking behind the brand.

If we simply ask AI to make the decisions for us, that personal touch can disappear.

## My approach

The solution isn't to stop using AI.

Instead, we need to change where AI fits into the design process.

The thought process should still belong to the designer.

Before opening an AI tool, we should understand the problem, the users, the brand, and the experience we are trying to create. We should make the important design decisions ourselves.

AI can then become a tool that supports that process rather than replacing it.

It can help us:

- Brainstorm ideas
- Explore different directions
- Find inspiration
- Learn about design theories
- Question our decisions
- Explore possibilities we may not have considered

But there is an important difference between using AI to explore an idea and asking AI to create the idea for you.

The first keeps the designer in control. The second can easily remove the designer from the process.


## Designing with a system, not a prompt

One of the best ways to keep personality in a design is to start with the company's existing design system.

A design system is more than a collection of colors, fonts, buttons, and components. It represents a set of decisions about how a brand communicates and how its products should feel.

When designing, we should use the elements that already resonate with the brand.

That could mean a particular typography system, spacing approach, color palette, visual language, interaction pattern, or component style.

The exact system will always depend on the company.

If your company already has a design system, follow it. If you're building one, make deliberate decisions about what makes the brand recognizable.

The goal isn't to make the design look different just for the sake of being different.

The goal is to make it feel intentional.

## Where AI should actually help

There is also an important distinction between design and development.

I don't think AI needs to disappear from the workflow. In fact, it can be extremely useful once the designer has established the vision.

Think about the process like this:

Designer → Vision → AI-assisted development → Final product

The designer establishes the experience, visual direction, design system, and important decisions first.

Then AI can help turn that vision into a working product.

At that point, the AI already has something meaningful to work from. Instead of generating a generic interface from a vague prompt, it is implementing a direction that has already been designed.

This gives us the speed of AI without necessarily sacrificing the personality of the product.


## How should designers balance AI and creativity?

I don't think the answer is to avoid AI.

Designers can use AI for inspiration, exploration, learning, and feedback.

For example, you can ask AI about a design theory you don't understand, explore different approaches to a problem, or get another perspective on a decision you've already made.

But the final decision should still come from you.

AI can give you options.

You decide which option is right.

AI can help you explore.

You decide where to go.

AI can help you build.

You define what should be built.

So the next time you use AI for a design task, ask yourself:
## Am I using AI to improve my thinking, or am I using it instead of thinking?
